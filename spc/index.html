Your shift starts now

Step 1 of 5 · Setup

Plant manager: "Line 4 has been fine all week. Get me a baseline before the truck comes."

You run the fill valve on Line 4. Hold it open about 3 seconds to fill a 300 mL bottle (it flows 100 mL per second). You can't see into the bottle, so you fill by feel. A scale weighs each tray of four bottles.

  1. Pick a recorder: worksheet, pen, calculator.
  2. Everyone else is an operator: enter your initials in seating order. Pass the valve after every bottle, around the circle, so each sample of 4 comes from 4 different people. The phone stays on the table.
  3. Feel only. No counting out loud, no stopwatch. Never adjust your hold because of the numbers: deciding whether the process changed is the chart's job, not the operator's.
  4. One phone for the whole shift. The data lives on it. No private tab.

Practice fill · target 300---

Warm-up: 3 practice fills each

Each operator takes 3 practice fills in a row, reading shown after each. Use these to find your 3 s: this is the only time you adjust. Once the baseline starts, the scale shows a full tray at a time and your hold stays put. Practice fills are not saved.

Step 3 of 5 · Limits

Split it: each operator computes the mean and range of one sample (sum ÷ 4, largest − smallest); one person does the two averages and the four limits; the recorder checks the worksheet against the log. Enter your six numbers and the station checks them.

(A plant would take 20 to 25 samples before trusting these limits; 8 is our classroom compromise, so expect them to be a little rough.)
